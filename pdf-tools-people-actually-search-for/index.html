PDF Tools People Actually Search For (and When to Use Them)

Most people searching for pdf tools want one of five things: merge files, split a long file, shrink it, convert it, or sign it. Everything else is noise. Here is each job, the fastest free way to do it, and the settings that decide whether the result is usable.

The five PDF jobs behind almost every search

Merge. Joining a stack of scans, invoices, or signed pages into one file. Highest-volume search of the lot.

Split and extract. Pulling out pages 4 to 7, or breaking a 300-page report into chapters. Also covers deleting and reordering pages.

Compress. Email limits are usually 10 to 25 MB, and a phone-scanned PDF is often 5 to 20 MB for a handful of pages, so this is the second most common fix after merging.

Convert. Two directions. PDF to Word or Excel when someone needs to edit; images, Word, or slides to PDF when someone needs to send. Conversion is where layout breaks, so it gets its own section below.

Sign and fill. Typed signature, date, checkboxes. For most ordinary contracts in the US and EU, a typed name plus clear intent is binding; a scanned squiggle is not more valid, just more familiar.

Second tier, searched less but urgently: OCR (making a scan searchable), password removal (unlocking a file you own), and redaction (actually deleting text, not drawing a black box over it).

How to do each one, step by step

Start with what you already have. Mac and Windows both ship a free PDF viewer that merges, reorders, deletes pages, fills forms, and signs. On a Mac, open the file and use the thumbnail sidebar: drag to reorder, select and press delete to remove, then print both files to one PDF to merge them. On Windows, the built-in browser viewer handles reading and filling, and the print-to-PDF route handles merging.

For everything else, a browser tool is fine:

  1. 1. Merge. Drop in all files, drag the thumbnails into order, export. Check the page count on the result before you send it.
  2. 2. Split. Choose ranges rather than "split every page" unless you truly want 300 files. Most tools accept input like 1-3, 8, 12-20.
  3. 3. Compress. Pick the middle setting first. Aggressive compression re-encodes images and turns crisp text into mush; medium usually cuts 60 to 80 percent with no visible change at reading size.
  4. 4. Convert. For PDF to Word, expect reflow: paragraphs survive, tables and multi-column layouts often do not. If the PDF is a scan, run OCR first or the output will be pictures of text.
  5. 5. Sign. Fill the fields, add a typed signature, then flatten or lock the file so the fields stop being editable. Save a copy; some viewers never warn you that the signature is still a movable object.

Two habits save the most time. Keep the original untouched and work on a copy. And name the output with a date and version, because "document-final-2.pdf" is how people send the wrong file.

Safety, quality, and when to stop using free tools

A PDF carries more than it looks like. Contracts, IDs, medical records, and payslips are the usual uploads, so treat any free site as a public place. Before uploading, check three things: whether the site deletes files automatically and after how long, whether that promise sits on a privacy page rather than a banner, and whether it demands an account. If a document contains a Social Security number, an account number, or a client's name, use software that runs on your own machine instead of a website.

Redaction is the one place people get burned. Drawing a black rectangle over text hides it from the eye and leaves it in the file, selectable and copyable. Use a real redaction tool that strips the underlying characters, then reopen the export and try to select that area to confirm it is gone.

Free tiers stop being free at predictable points: files over 25 to 100 MB, more than a couple of jobs a day, batch work, or OCR. If you hit those limits weekly, paid desktop software pays for itself. If you hit them twice a year, split the file and carry on.

Finally, quality check before sending. Zoom to 100 percent and read a paragraph. If the text is fuzzy, the compression was too aggressive. If the page count changed, the merge dropped something. Ten seconds of checking beats resending a contract.

FAQ

What is the best free PDF tool?

The one already on your computer. Built-in viewers merge, reorder, delete pages, fill forms, and sign for free. Use a browser tool for compression and format conversion, and read its file-deletion policy first.

How do I merge PDFs without uploading them?

Use your system viewer's print-to-PDF option or a desktop utility that runs offline. Both keep the file on your machine. Browser-based tools send your document to a server to do the same work.

Why does my converted PDF look wrong?

PDF stores text by position on the page, not by paragraph. Converters have to guess the structure back, and tables and multi-column layouts are where the guess fails. Scans need OCR before conversion or the output is just images.

How much can I compress a PDF without losing quality?

Usually 60 to 80 percent at medium settings, because most of the savings come from re-encoding images. If text looks fuzzy at 100 percent zoom, step back one compression level.

Are typed signatures legally valid?

For most ordinary contracts in the US and EU, yes. Intent plus a record of who signed and when matters more than the image itself. Check the specific document if it involves real estate, wills, or regulated filings.
